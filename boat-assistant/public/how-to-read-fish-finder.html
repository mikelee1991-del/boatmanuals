What you are looking at

Traditional / CHIRP sonar scrolls newest data on the right, history to the left. Depth is usually a large number at the top. Between the surface and the bottom you see the water column — clutter, bait, game fish, and structure.

  • Top band — surface clutter from waves, bubbles, and prop wash. Ignore most of it; turn gain down if it eats the screen.
  • Middle — suspended targets (fish, bait) and thermoclines (often a faint horizontal band).
  • Bottom line — the strongest continuous return. Thickness and color tell you hardness and cover.

Multi-spectrum bands — blue and black

Colors are not “fish species.” They map return strength — how hard the echo bounced back to the transducer. On Garmin-style traditional palettes (and many “blue” schemes), think of a spectrum from weak to strong:

Blue / dark cool
weak return Mid tones
moderate Black / hot bright
strong return

Blue band

Weak echo. Open water, soft mud/silt edges, light weed, small bait, or a fish off to the side of the cone. Blue filling most of the column usually means “not much solid down there.”

Fishing: soft bottom + scattered blue flecks → search for bait clouds or move until you find a harder edge where fish stage.

Black / strongest band

Dense, hard echo. Rock, compacted sand/gravel, wrecks, thick shell, and solid fish marks near the center of the beam. On some Garmin views the strongest bottom paint is black (or bright white/yellow/red depending on palette) — treat it as “maximum intensity,” not a literal color of the seabed.

Fishing: black/hard transitions next to softer blue/green ground are prime. Predators sit on the hard edge and feed into the soft.

Palette names differ (“Garmin,” “Blue,” “Amber,” etc.). Always read the unit’s color legend: cool/dark = weak, hot/bright or solid black = strong. If you change palette, the fishing logic stays the same; only the paint changes.

Reading bottom type

What you see Likely bottom Fishing note
Thin, faint, soft-edged line (often blue/cool) Mud, silt, soft sand Flats and soft basins; look for bait clouds above, not hard cover
Thick, sharp, intense line (black / yellow / red / white) Rock, gravel, hard sand, shell Structure holds; cast or drop along the hard edge
Fuzzy “hair” or fuzzy rise off the bottom Weed / grass / soft cover Weed edges and holes in the grass hold fish
Sudden spike or step in the bottom contour Ledge, reef, wreck, rock pile Mark a waypoint; work the up-current or shady side
Second echo (ghost bottom below the first) Very hard bottom (signal bounced twice) Useful hardness clue in shallow hard ground

Whiteline / bottom fill (if your GPSMAP sonar menu offers it) paints the strongest part of the bottom return so thickness is easier to read: thick highlight = harder; thin = softer.

Fish, bait, and false marks

  • Arches — classic traditional mark. A fish swimming through the cone: weak on the edge, strong in the middle, weak again. Full thick arches are centered under the boat; hooks and dashes are edge-of-cone or fast boat speed.
  • Color of the arch — stronger (black / hot) usually means denser target or better beam center, not automatically a bigger species. Blue/cool arches are often small bait, soft returns, or fish on the cone edge.
  • Bait — clouds, smears, or speckled patches mid-column. Dense packs paint hotter; loose shiners look blue/gray and airy.
  • Thermocline — a faint horizontal band across the column. Game fish often sit just above or below it; bait stacks there too.
  • Fish symbols — optional icons. Handy for beginners, but they guess. Learn arches and clouds; turn symbols off once you are comfortable.

Traditional vs ClearVü on this Garmin

This Flyer 8 helm runs a Garmin GPSMAP x3 chartplotter/sonar (exact 7″ / 9″ / 12″ and xsv SKU still verify under Settings → System → About). With a compatible transducer (likely GT23-class), you typically get:

  • Traditional / CHIRP — conical beam, arches, best for depth, hardness, and fish in the water column while you idle or troll.
  • ClearVü — narrow downward scan, more photo-like. Fish look like dots or dashes, not arches. Structure, brush, and bottom texture are clearer. Use it to confirm what traditional found.

Split the screen (chart + sonar, or traditional + ClearVü) the way the owner photos show. Idle under ~5–6 kn for readable ClearVü detail; traditional stays usable a bit faster.

On-the-water settings that help

  1. Start with Auto Gain, then bump gain until you see light speckles in open water — then back off one notch.
  2. Use Color Gain / Contrast after gain: higher makes small targets pop; too high washes out bottom hardness.
  3. Pick a palette you can read in sun (often a blue or high-contrast scheme), then stick with it so your brain learns the spectrum.
  4. When you find a hard edge next to soft blue ground with bait above it — mark the waypoint and fish that transition.
  5. No depth / flashing depth digits → boat may be too fast, transducer kicked up, or sonar source wrong. See Ask the boat / troubleshooting for MFD faults.
