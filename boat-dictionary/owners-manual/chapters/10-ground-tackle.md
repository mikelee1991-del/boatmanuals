## How deep can I anchor? (scope vs rode)

**Short answer:** This binder does **not** yet have a measured rode length (chain + rope) for HIN `BEYFT208F223`. Without that number, there is **no boat-specific maximum anchoring depth** to quote as fact.

What you *can* use today:

1. **Draft / approach depth (CONFIRMED range):** stay in water deeper than the hull draft with margin — treat **≥1.0–1.5 m** under the keel as a cautious shallow-water floor until you know local conditions and tide. Draft is **0.50–0.90 m**; do not confuse that with rode length.
2. **Scope (general seamanship — not a Beneteau-spec number):** common coastal guidance is roughly **5:1** rode:depth in calm conditions and **7:1+** when wind/wave pick up (some use ~3:1 overnight only with all-chain and good holding — treat that as advanced, not a default).  
   - Example: **5 m** water + **1 m** freeboard to bow roller ≈ **6 m** “depth for scope” → at **5:1** you want about **30 m** of rode out.
3. **Max depth limited by rode on board (UNVERIFIED until measured):**  
   `max practical depth ≈ (usable rode length ÷ chosen scope) − bow height`.  
   Photograph or measure total chain + rope (meters/feet) and add it to this chapter.
4. **Garmin depth** is water under the transducer — useful for choosing a spot; it does **not** tell you how much rode you have left on the gypsy.
5. **Windlass** hauls and pays rode; it is **not** a depth rating. Breaker trips / dead windlass → `OM-TS-WINDLASS`, not a depth question.

## Safe operation checklist
1. Engine in neutral; bow clear of people.
2. HOUSE/ENGINE power ON; windlass breaker ON.
3. Do not rely on windlass as sole cleat — snub rode to bow cleat when set.
4. When retrieving, motor slowly toward anchor; do not yank with windlass alone against a set anchor.
5. Seat anchor firmly in roller for underway; latch if equipped.
6. If breaker trips repeatedly: check amp draw, fouled gypsy, low voltage.

Faults → `OM-TS-WINDLASS`.
